# Design history: what was tried and why it was rejected

## v1: pastel and rounded (rejected: "very LLM/AI")
Fredoka font, pastel radial-gradient blobs on cream, rounded white cards with offset shadows, emoji icons, bouncing title letters and a wiggling "NEW!" pill. This is the default AI-made "kid site" template.

## v2: fridge door (rejected: "meh", and too young)
A retro mint fridge with draggable plastic alphabet magnets, toys shown as crayon drawings on notebook, graph and construction paper held up with tape or magnets, gold-star stickers for favourites, and a sticky note from Dad. Patrick Hand + Lilita One fonts, SVG crayon and wobble filters, hatch-pattern fills.
- It worked technically: the magnet layout re-stacked on phones and positions persisted.
- Why it failed:
  1. It was aimed at preschoolers.
  2. The skeuomorphic props were a costume that upstaged the toys.
  3. It wasn't what the user had in mind.
- Process lesson: the user wrote "...meh. still. hmmm / like the design", and it was misread as approval when it meant "the design is meh too". When feedback is ambiguous, ask.

## The key correction
The audience is **all ages** (neal.fun: kids and adults both get hooked), not small children. Monster Maker is still wanted as a toy for younger kids (about 6). See `project-context.md`.

## Round 3: neutral all-ages mocks (rejected: "don't like any of that")
Three directions: Editorial (serif, warm), Poster blocks (Bricolage, colour blocks), Night lab (dark, mono). Names: Rabbit Holes, Small Wonders, and so on. All were too safe and too conventional.
The user asked for something "more creative" and **arcade-like**.

## Round 4: arcade (in review)
- A: an arcade floor on neon carpet. Each toy is a cabinet with a lit marquee, a CRT screen playing a pixel attract loop, a control panel and a "FREE PLAY" coin door. "Coming soon" is an out-of-order machine with static.
- B: a single big CRT game-select menu, with a blinking-cursor list on the left and the selected game's attract loop on the right.
- Fonts: Monoton (neon), Bungee / Bungee Inline (marquees), VT323 and Silkscreen (screens).
- The attract loops are tiny canvases drawn at 64×48 and scaled up pixelated, so they're cheap and need no image assets.
